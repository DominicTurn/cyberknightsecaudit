The Risk

Your domain may be easier to impersonate than you think.

Email authentication records are public DNS records. If they are missing, duplicated, outdated, or too permissive, attackers may have an easier path to spoof your organization.

Fake emails can look real.

A criminal can use your name, your domain, and urgent language to target donors, staff, vendors, or board members.

Trust is part of your security posture.

Nonprofits depend on reputation. A single impersonation attempt can create donor confusion and board-level concern.

DNS settings change over time.

New email platforms, CRMs, fundraising tools, and vendors can leave old or conflicting records behind.

What We Review

One focused audit. Four critical areas.

The audit is narrow by design. We inspect the records that control who is allowed to send email for your domain and how receiving mail servers should treat suspicious messages.

01 / SPF

Sender Policy Framework

We check whether your SPF record exists, is valid, has duplicate entries, exceeds lookup limits, or allows too many senders.

02 / DKIM

DomainKeys Identified Mail

We verify DKIM alignment and identify whether your email platforms are properly signing outbound messages.

03 / DMARC

DMARC Policy

We review your DMARC record, policy level, alignment settings, reporting destinations, and enforcement readiness.

04 / DNS

Email DNS Records

We inspect MX, TXT, CNAME and related records that affect email authentication, deliverability, and domain trust.
