02 / Audit Scope

The Audit Is Based Primarily on Public DNS Information

The Starter Email Authentication Audit is primarily a read-only review of publicly available domain and email-authentication records.

Depending on the agreed scope, the review may include:

  • SPF records and basic SPF syntax.
  • DKIM selectors that are provided by the client or commonly associated with identified email providers.
  • DMARC policy, reporting, and alignment settings.
  • MX, TXT, nameserver, and related DNS records.
  • Mail-provider identification and domain-configuration observations.
  • A plain-English executive report and prioritized recommendations.

Unless expressly agreed in writing, the audit does not include penetration testing, mailbox access, password testing, malware analysis, employee monitoring, internal account review, vulnerability exploitation, or review of private systems.

03 / Findings

Findings Are Informational

Audit findings, scores, recommendations, and risk classifications are provided for informational and decision-support purposes. They are based on the records, configuration data, documentation, and client-provided information available at the time of review.

Findings are not legal advice, regulatory certification, insurance advice, financial advice, or a guarantee of compliance with any law, grant requirement, contract, cybersecurity framework, or industry standard.

The client remains responsible for evaluating recommendations in the context of its own systems, vendors, risk tolerance, contractual obligations, and operational needs.

04 / Threat Coverage

No Guarantee That All Threats Will Be Identified

Cyber Knight Sec does not guarantee that an audit will identify every security weakness, email-delivery problem, spoofing method, phishing campaign, unauthorized sender, misconfiguration, or future threat.

Public DNS records provide only a limited view of an organization’s security posture. Risks may exist in private systems, user accounts, vendor configurations, endpoint devices, cloud platforms, mail-flow rules, or internal procedures that are outside the agreed scope.

No audit can guarantee that a domain will never be spoofed, that every fraudulent message will be blocked, or that every legitimate message will be delivered.

05 / Authorization

The Client Must Approve All DNS Changes

Cyber Knight Sec will not publish, modify, delete, or replace a DNS record without authorization from the domain owner or an authorized administrator.

Before any implementation work begins, the client must approve the proposed change, the affected record, and the expected purpose of the change. The client is responsible for confirming that the person granting approval has authority over the domain and related systems.

DNS changes may affect email delivery, vendor integrations, website services, marketing platforms, fundraising tools, or other connected systems. The client must disclose known authorized senders and service providers before enforcement policies are strengthened.

DNS propagation, caching, registrar limitations, third-party outages, and vendor-specific behavior may delay or affect results after a change is made.
